# Doodle distribution routes

Research date: 2026-09-11

Doodle is a small web app that turns a short description into a simple drawing idea for a card, lunchbox note, or journal. The current product offer is two anonymous generations free, followed by one-time packs. The goal here is intent distribution with no ad budget and almost no existing audience. A listing or accepted pitch creates an opportunity for discovery. It does not guarantee traffic, users, or sales.

## 1. Product Hunt, self-serve launch

**Recommendation: execute first after the live product and gallery have passed render QA.**

**Why this is a fit.** Product Hunt has a current, searchable Drawing topic. The page includes drawing apps, AI generative media, art tools, and kids' creativity products. It also displays a design tool forum thread marked 9 months old with 6 comments and 148 views. A separate drawing product page crawled four days ago shows 76 followers, 94 points, and a maker asking for feedback. This is direct evidence that people use the catalog for drawing related tools, although it is not evidence that Doodle will rank or receive a particular number of visits. Sources: [Product Hunt Drawing topic](https://www.producthunt.com/topics/drawing), [Bit Sketchy drawing product page](https://www.producthunt.com/products/bit-sketchy-party-game).

**Submission mechanism and cost.** Use a personal, free Product Hunt account and [Submit a new product](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/preparing-for-launch): log in, select Submit, then New Product, and enter the clean product URL `https://doodle.samistudio.nl`. Product Hunt says regular launches are free, company accounts cannot post, and a newly created personal account normally needs one week before posting. The launch can be scheduled up to one month ahead. Product Hunt rejects shortened and UTM tracking links in the submission URL. Sources: [Product Hunt posting guide](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/preparing-for-launch), [Product Hunt Help Center, access to post](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/481909-how-can-i-get-access-to-post), [Product Hunt, how it works](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/how-product-hunt-works).

**Fields to prepare.**

- Name: `Doodle`
- Tagline: `Little drawing ideas for cards and notes`
- Description: `Doodle turns one small moment into a simple drawing you can copy by hand or share. Try your first two doodles free without signing in, for a birthday card, lunchbox note, journal page, or thank-you. After that, buy a one-time pack when you want more ideas.`
- Launch tags: `Drawing`, `Art`, `AI Generative Media` (use the closest tags shown by the form)
- Pricing: `Paid with free trial or plan`
- Product URL: `https://doodle.samistudio.nl`
- Thumbnail and gallery: one square thumbnail plus at least two clear, readable examples. Show the prompt or moment, the resulting drawing idea, and the card, note, or journal use case. Do not imply the generated example was drawn by hand.

**Prepared first comment.**

> Hi Product Hunt. I made Doodle for the small moments where you want to draw something personal but the blank page gets in the way. Describe one moment, such as a dog in a party hat or two people dancing in the kitchen, and Doodle gives you a simple starting point for a card, lunchbox note, or journal. The first two doodles are free without signing in. I would especially value feedback from people who use an example as a reference and then add their own linework.

Product Hunt's own guide recommends a maker first comment that explains the audience, process, and pricing and asks for feedback rather than upvotes. The guide reports that 70% of products reaching Product of the Day, Week, or Month had a maker first comment. That statistic is a launch practice signal, not a forecast for Doodle. Source: [Product Hunt first comment guidance](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/preparing-for-launch).

**Policy and risk.** The Drawing topic already contains AI drawing and AI generative media products, so the category fit is credible. Keep the copy concrete and disclose the product's generated output in the gallery or description if the final UI makes that necessary. Do not pay a hunter or buy votes. The main audience mismatch is that Product Hunt's core audience is makers and early adopters, while Doodle's end use is cards, notes, and journals.

**Realistic expectation.** One launch can plausibly produce a small burst of qualified clicks, feedback, and a few trial users if the gallery makes the use case obvious. There is no basis for promising sustained reach or purchases. Measure visits, completed first generations, and pack checkout starts from the launch date in the existing analytics. Submit only after the live site has been manually checked on mobile and desktop.

## 2. Future Tools, free curated AI tool submission

**Recommendation: a low-effort secondary listing experiment after Product Hunt, pending authorization to publish externally.**

**Why this is a fit.** Future Tools has a live, searchable `Generative Art` category. The current category page shows 293 tools across 12 pages, including free and freemium visual tools, AI illustration, image generation, and prompt-based creative products. That is a genuine current catalog in the exact discovery area Doodle would enter. It is still a directory, so catalog presence is not proof of reach or placement. Source: [Future Tools Generative Art category](https://www.futuretools.io/tools?categories=generative-art).

**Exact submission mechanism and cost.** Use the live [Future Tools Submit a Tool form](https://futuretools.io/submit-a-tool). The official form asks for: Your Name (optional), Tool Name (required), Tool URL (required), Short Description (required), Category (required), Pricing (required: Free, Freemium, Paid, or Open Source), Your Email (required), optional newsletter opt-in, and a CAPTCHA. No submission fee or paid requirement is shown on the form. Future Tools says the site is maintained so it stays free for people looking for tools and for people submitting tools. Sources: [Future Tools submission form](https://futuretools.io/submit-a-tool), [Future Tools FAQ](https://futuretools.io/faq).

**Prepared fields.**

- Your Name: `Sami`
- Tool Name: `Doodle`
- Tool URL: `https://doodle.samistudio.nl`
- Short Description: `Simple drawing ideas for cards, lunchbox notes, and journals. Try two generations free, then buy one-time packs.`
- Category: `Generative Art`
- Pricing: `Freemium`
- Your Email: owner's approved project email
- Newsletter opt-in: leave unchecked unless wanted

**Policy and risk.** Future Tools says submissions are manually reviewed and that more than 75% of submissions are not approved. Its rejection reasons include a low-effort or poor user experience, an unavailable product, and a submission that is not actually a software tool. Doodle is publicly usable and is a software tool, so it clears those stated baseline conditions if the live page renders well. Generated art is within the category, but the final listing may be summarized by the site's AI and then human reviewed. Verify that the homepage plainly says drawing ideas for cards, notes, and journals before submitting. Source: [Future Tools FAQ, review and rejection criteria](https://futuretools.io/faq).

**Realistic expectation.** The cost is zero and the form takes a few minutes, which makes this worth one careful submission. Approval is unlikely relative to a normal directory because Future Tools reports a greater than 75% rejection rate, and there is no stated turnaround. If accepted, Doodle will be discoverable beside AI visual tools, but the audience is AI tool seekers rather than card and journaling users. Do not buy a featured placement or treat approval as evidence of product demand.

## Routes deliberately rejected

- [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/faq/) is searchable and has drawing categories, but its current FAQ says it generally does not approve basic AI tools and AI image, video, or music generators. Doodle is a poor policy fit.
- Feeling Creative is rejected for this purpose. Its current [submission rules](https://feeling-creative.com/write-for-us) explicitly say it does not publish promotional articles written on behalf of a business, brand, or product. Do not invent a founder story or reframe a product pitch as a creator spotlight to bypass that rule.
- Reddit drawing and craft threads can show user need, but a reply containing a product link would be unsolicited outreach and community rules vary. They remain observation sources, not a publishing route under the current authorization.

## Execution order

1. Finish live mobile and desktop render QA, then assemble the Product Hunt thumbnail and two gallery images.
2. Create or use a personal Product Hunt account, respecting the one-week posting requirement, and save the launch as a draft. Do not launch until the owner approves the final rendered preview and copy.
3. Prepare the Future Tools form with the fields above, then submit once only after the owner authorizes external publishing. Choose `Generative Art` and record whether it is accepted. Do not resubmit before a week if the FAQ's waiting guidance applies.
